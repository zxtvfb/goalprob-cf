How the model works

1 · Team profile. Every team keeps a rolling window of its last 45 matches. From those: goals scored and conceded per game, BTTS rate, clean sheets, scored-in-both-halves, win/draw/loss split, and first-half results — each also split by home and away.

2 · Attack & defence strength. Each rate is divided by its league average to give a strength index. 1.00 is league-average; 1.30 means 30% above. Teams with few matches are shrunk toward the league mean so a 5-game sample can't produce a wild number.

3 · Expected goals. Home λ = home attack × away defence × league home average. Away λ the same way. A separate pair of λ values is built for the first half from halftime scores only.

4 · Score matrix. A Poisson grid over 0–8 goals each side gives the probability of every scoreline. Summing the cells gives 1X2, BTTS, over/under and correct score. A Dixon-Coles correction lifts low-scoring draws, which raw Poisson underrates.

Caveat. The model sees goals, not injuries, suspensions, motivation, weather or rotation. It is a baseline to price against, not a tip.
